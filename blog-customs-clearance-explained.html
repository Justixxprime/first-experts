What customs clearance actually involves.

Demystifying the step that causes most shipping delays.

6 min read

Customs clearance is the process of getting goods legally authorised to enter or leave a country. It sits between "your cargo has arrived" and "your cargo is actually released to you," and it's where most avoidable shipping delays happen.

The process, broadly

A clearance agent reviews your shipment's documentation, classifies the goods under the correct tariff code, calculates and settles any applicable duties, and coordinates with the port or airport authority to have the cargo released. Done correctly the first time, this can move quickly. Done with missing or incorrect paperwork, it can stall for days.

Most customs delays trace back to paperwork, not the cargo itself.

What usually causes delay

Missing invoices, an incorrect or vague goods description, a mismatch between declared and actual value, or simply not having a clearance agent engaged before the cargo arrives. None of these are about the cargo being problematic. They're almost always about the paperwork not being ready before it's needed.

Why a licensed agent matters

A licensed clearance agent knows the correct classification codes, current duty rates, and documentation standards for the port or airport your cargo is moving through, and has an existing working relationship with that authority. This is the difference between a straightforward clearance and one that requires back-and-forth correction after the fact.

What "cleared" actually means

Once cargo is cleared, it has been legally authorised for release and any duties owed have been settled. It doesn't necessarily mean the cargo is already at your door. Delivery is usually a separate step, which is why customs clearance and last-mile delivery are often bundled together, as they are in our Door to Door service.

Preparing for a smoother clearance

Have your commercial invoice, packing list, and any required certificates ready before your cargo arrives, and give your clearance agent accurate information rather than a rough estimate.
